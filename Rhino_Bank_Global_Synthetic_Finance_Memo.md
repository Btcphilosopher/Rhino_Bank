# RHINO BANK — INTERNAL MEMORANDUM

**Date:** 16 November 2026  
**To:** Board of Directors; Group Treasury; RhinoQuant; Structured Finance Committee  
**From:** Group Strategy — Global Synthetic Finance Programme  
**Subject:** Project World Ledger: financing the global economy through Rhino-originated obligations and synthetic CDOs  
**Status:** Hypothetical strategy and indicative prospectus framework. No securities are being offered; no institutional participation, licence, rating or guarantee is represented as secured.

**The proposition**

Rhino Bank could aspire to become the organising institution through which the world's finance is originated, priced, divided into risk classes and distributed. Its opportunity would be to standardise the financial claims supporting economic activity: business loans, infrastructure debt, trade receivables, housing finance and sovereign obligations. A common system could make these claims easier to finance and hedge while matching their risks to institutions able to bear them.

In the terminal scenario requested for this memorandum, all eligible global financing passes through Rhino's infrastructure, and a consortium of commercial banks, central banks and international financial institutions purchases its securities. This is a counterfactual institutional arrangement, not a statement of existing demand or powers. The commercial prize is control of the origination and servicing franchise, alongside whatever economic interests Rhino retains.

The distinction is fundamental: arranging every contract would not confer ownership of the world economy. Selling every tranche would transfer the associated economic exposure to investors. Rhino could own the platform and earn fees while other institutions own the securities.

**GDP as the scale of ambition**

The IMF's October 2026 DataMapper displays 2026 world nominal GDP at approximately **US$128 trillion**, rounded. This memorandum uses that figure as a sizing anchor, not a measurement of financeable assets. Nominal GDP is an annual flow of value added. It is neither global wealth nor an inventory of assignable cash flows, and it is not an annual borrowing requirement. [1]

Define the reference portfolio as P = G × e × c × m, where G is annual nominal GDP, e is the share associated with eligible financing, c is Rhino's share of that financing, and m is the assumed ratio of outstanding principal to one year's eligible activity. For illustration only, set e = 100% and m = 1. This produces the following sensitivity:

| Rhino coverage c | Outstanding reference principal P | Annual principal originated/replaced at 20% turnover |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1% | $1.28tn | $256bn |
| 10% | $12.8tn | $2.56tn |
| 100% | $128tn | $25.6tn |

The 100% case is the ambition's upper-bound thought experiment. It assumes a legally enforceable debt portfolio equal to one year's world output. It does not establish that such a portfolio exists or can be assembled. The turnover assumption implies five years of principal outstanding per unit of annual originations in steady state; it is not a forecast of transaction volumes or a claim about contractual maturity.

Eligibility, debt balances, contract duration, currencies and duplication would need to be established from actual loan-level data. Loans, supplier receivables and sovereign obligations cannot simply be added together and labelled GDP without checking overlapping exposures and repayment sources.

**What would actually be sold**

The programme requires two distinct mechanisms.

First, a funded lending and cash-securitisation channel. Rhino or partner lenders advance money against enforceable borrower obligations. Bankruptcy-remote vehicles purchase eligible loans and issue funded CDO notes. Investors' subscription proceeds pay for those assets, potentially replenishing the lenders' capacity. Borrowers' interest and principal support the securities. This is the channel capable of supplying or refinancing money for the real economy.

Second, a synthetic credit-risk channel. Rhino retains specified loans while transferring defined credit losses through derivatives or guarantees, potentially supported by funded credit-linked notes. This reflects the basic structure of synthetic risk transfer described by the BIS. [2] A synthetic CDO references obligations; it does not ordinarily purchase those obligations or give its investors title to the factories, homes or national output behind them.

In a fully funded synthetic structure covering the entire illustrative $128tn portfolio, aggregate investor collateral could reach $128tn. But that collateral secures contingent loss payments. It is not automatically another $128tn available for lending. Its permitted investment, custody and use must be specified. If the programme instead protects only a $12.8tn slice, funded principal can be $12.8tn against a $128tn reference portfolio. The remaining $115.2tn of portfolio risk stays elsewhere.

Cash CDO funding and synthetic protection can coexist, but their cash flows and risk transfer must be reported separately. Counting both as new finance would overstate the programme. Repeated synthetic contracts on the same debt can also increase gross notional without increasing productive investment.

For illustration, partition the $128tn reference principal into the following loss bands. These are arithmetic risk bands, not credit ratings or predictions of investor demand.

| Tranche | Portfolio loss band | Share | Principal |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Equity / first loss | 0–5% | 5% | $6.4tn |
| Mezzanine | 5–15% | 10% | $12.8tn |
| Junior senior | 15–30% | 15% | $19.2tn |
| Senior | 30–100% | 70% | $89.6tn |
| Total | 0–100% | 100% | $128tn |

Net credit losses exhaust the lowest band first. Available interest pays contractual expenses and note coupons in the documented order, with excess spread and principal subject to triggers. No rating follows merely from being senior: global recession, collateral impairment, legal failure and correlated sovereign defaults can defeat seemingly substantial protection.

**Who buys the obligations?**

Commercial banks are plausible counterparties in principle, but every purchase creates exposure and consumes capacity under the applicable prudential regime. If originating banks buy one another's risk through Rhino vehicles, the banking system may merely redistribute losses internally. Financing buyers of Rhino's own notes can reconnect risk to the originating balance sheet. BIS analysis identifies such feedback risks in synthetic risk-transfer markets. [2]

The World Bank must be treated through its distinct institutional arms and mandates. IBRD's 2025 statements describe a liquidity portfolio prioritising principal protection and high-quality instruments. [3] That is not evidence of permission or appetite to absorb a global Rhino CDO programme. Any proposed participation would require a specific eligible investment, guarantee or development-finance mandate and approval.

The IMF's stated lending role is temporary balance-of-payments support rather than project financing. [4] It cannot be assumed to function as a commercial CDO purchaser. In this memorandum's universal-financing scenario, IMF purchases require a new and explicitly authorised institutional arrangement; they are excluded from the executable commercial demand case.

Central-bank participation likewise remains a counterfactual assumption until the relevant purchase powers, eligible assets and approvals are established. A note accepted as collateral is not thereby purchased outright. Broad official purchases could place private credit losses onto public balance sheets and create monetary, fiscal and governance consequences requiring separate assessment.

Accordingly, the prospectus cannot describe any of these institutions as committed buyers. Universal participation is a scenario condition, not underwriting evidence.

**The economics of owning the franchise**

Rhino's scalable earnings would come from origination, structuring, administration, servicing, analytics and execution. The following arithmetic assumes a 10-basis-point annual administration fee on outstanding principal, a 20-basis-point arrangement fee on annual originations, and the 20% turnover assumption above. Each fee is charged once at programme level, not on every layer of the same exposure.

| Coverage | Annual administration revenue | Annual arrangement revenue | Total gross annual fee revenue |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1% | $1.28bn | $0.512bn | $1.792bn |
| 10% | $12.8bn | $5.12bn | $17.92bn |
| 100% | $128bn | $51.2bn | $179.2bn |

These are sensitivities, not forecasts. They exclude operating costs, funding costs, hedging, credit losses, taxes, retained capital and pricing pressure. A $128tn reference portfolio would not make Rhino worth $128tn. Its equity value would depend on distributable earnings, capital requirements, retained liabilities and the durability of its franchise. Notes sold at par generate subscription proceeds matched by obligations, not an equivalent profit.

Rhino must choose what it means by “own all the synthetic finance.” Owning the operating infrastructure can coexist with selling securities. Owning every tranche means retaining the exposures and obtaining funding elsewhere. Selling every tranche surrenders their economic returns and may still leave servicing, derivative, legal or reputational liabilities. Some jurisdictions or transaction types may require risk retention, preventing a literal sale of every economic interest.

**What the potential amounts to**

At its best, the model would turn fragmented financing into a more legible market. RhinoQuant could price currency, maturity, default and correlation risks consistently; borrowers could reach a wider capital base; investors could select exposures without originating every underlying loan. Matched funding and credible risk transfer might reduce some financing frictions. Whether borrowers actually receive lower rates would depend on the full cost of protection, hedging, capital and intermediation.

Pulling future income forward can accelerate productive projects, but repayment still comes from future cash generation. Synthetic structuring does not create the labour, materials, energy or savings required to build the assets. At worldwide scale, there is no external economy to absorb the system's aggregate losses: the holders of the contracts ultimately bear them, or governments choose to redistribute them.

**Stress and institutional concentration**

On the illustrative waterfall, 3% net portfolio losses equal $3.84tn and consume 60% of equity. At 10%, losses of $12.8tn wipe out equity and half the mezzanine tranche. At 20%, losses of $25.6tn wipe out equity and mezzanine and consume one-third of junior senior. At 35%, losses of $44.8tn reach the senior band and consume approximately 7.14% of its principal. These are cumulative credit-loss examples, not GDP declines or forecasts; they exclude market-price changes, liquidity calls, collateral losses and expenses.

Universal Rhino intermediation would concentrate operational, pricing, cyber, legal and political power. A common platform could become a common point of failure. Global diversification cannot eliminate a global shock. Resolution planning, independent custody, portable servicing, transparent exposure registers, independent valuation and limits on circular financing therefore belong in the operating model itself.

**Board recommendation**

Retain Project World Ledger as the strategic horizon, but authorise development around auditable, segmented portfolios with demonstrable funding needs and identified buyers. Scale should follow enforceability, realised performance and investor capacity. Before issuing any series, management should establish actual eligible assets, cash movements, investor mandates, capital treatment, stressed liquidity and the location of every retained loss.

Rhino's strongest ambition is to own the financial infrastructure through which productive claims can be financed and their risks distributed. The world could be financed through that infrastructure in the hypothetical scenario; its output could not be conjured into existence by securitisation. The franchise earns its value by improving the allocation of capital and bearing explicitly priced responsibilities.
