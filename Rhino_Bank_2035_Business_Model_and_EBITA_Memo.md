# RHINO BANK — INTERNAL MEMORANDUM

**Date:** 16 November 2026  
**To:** Board of Directors; Group Treasury; RhinoQuant; Structured Finance  
**From:** Group Strategy and Financial Planning  
**Subject:** Project World Ledger 2035 — business model for a $200 trillion global synthetic-finance franchise  
**Classification:** Internal strategic scenario; illustrative financial projections, not an offering document or representation of secured business.

**Executive position**

Under the stipulated scenario, Rhino Bank becomes the principal arranger, administrator and distributor of a global financing system with **$200 trillion of average outstanding reference obligations in 2035**. A fee model consistent with our earlier memorandum produces **$280 billion of annual net fee revenue and $142 billion of modelled EBITA**, a 50.7% margin after operating expenses, depreciation and a $20 billion planning charge for risk.

These figures are conditional on extraordinary adoption, continued investor capacity and unusually disciplined risk distribution. They describe the economics if the franchise exists; they do not establish the probability of attaining it. The $200tn figure is the user's scenario input, not a forecast of 2035 global GDP or a measured addressable market.

**1. The business Rhino owns**

Rhino owns the contractual and technological infrastructure connecting borrowers, originating lenders, financing vehicles and investors. RhinoQuant supplies portfolio analytics, pricing and risk surveillance. Rhino earns contracted fees for arranging transactions, administering obligations, servicing portfolios and coordinating distribution.

The group operates through local lending partners and separately documented issuance vehicles. It standardises data and contracts across the network while keeping country, currency, sector and legal exposures identifiable. Investors select risk bands rather than accepting an undifferentiated claim on the world economy.

The central commercial principle is to retain ownership of the franchise while distributing most investment risk. A $200tn reference portfolio is not $200tn of Rhino assets, revenue or equity value. Investors own the notes they purchase. Loans held by partner banks remain their assets unless sold; any controlled vehicles and guarantees require proper consolidation and exposure assessment.

**2. Funding and synthetic protection**

Underlying borrowers receive money from lenders or funded cash-securitisation vehicles. Rhino then arranges synthetic credit-risk transfer where suitable, with clearly documented credit events, attachment points, collateral, loss settlement and recourse. BIS describes synthetic risk transfers as arrangements that transfer credit risk while underlying loans remain owned by the originating bank. [1]

The model records $200tn once as the average unique reference principal administered. It excludes duplicated synthetic overlays and does not add loan balances, derivative notionals and investor collateral together. Synthetic contracts redistribute risk; they do not automatically provide a second source of loan funding.

The full reference portfolio may pass through the platform without every dollar of portfolio principal being covered by funded synthetic notes. If only selected risk bands are transferred, actual protection notional and note issuance must be reported separately. For simplicity, the arrangement-fee model below charges on newly arranged reference principal, not on the smaller principal of protected tranches.

The consortium envisaged in the earlier scenario includes commercial banks, central banks, the World Bank and the IMF. Their participation remains a counterfactual institutional assumption, dependent on relevant mandates, legal authority and approvals. No official-sector purchases, guarantees or backstops are treated as committed.

**3. Revenue architecture**

All fees below are net revenue attributable to Rhino after contractual partner shares, rebates and pass-through amounts. The fee schedule is assumed, not observed. Fees can differ by transaction; the table models portfolio-wide averages. There is no separate unmodelled revenue from collateral interest, trading gains, software sales or financing spreads.

| Revenue stream | Charging basis | 2035 assumption | Annual net revenue |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Portfolio administration and servicing | Average unique reference principal | 10bp × $200tn | $200bn |
| Structuring and distribution | Newly arranged reference principal | 20bp × $40tn | $80bn |
| **Total** | **14bp equivalent on average principal** | | **$280bn** |

One basis point on $200tn equals $20bn. The 10bp administration fee is charged once at programme level. The 20bp arrangement fee is charged once on annual reference principal arranged; it is not repeated across every tranche or intermediary.

The model assumes $40tn of annual arrangements: 20% of average outstanding principal. To make that flow compatible with continued expansion, the illustrative 2035 opening balance is $190tn, additions are $40tn and runoff is $20tn, giving a closing balance of $210tn and a simple average of $200tn. This assumes net portfolio growth of $20tn, with additions and runoff distributed evenly through the year. If actual runoff requires more than $40tn of arrangements to reach that closing balance, issuance capacity and associated costs must be re-modelled; incremental fees are not assumed here.

At an average $200tn reference balance, every extra basis point of synthetic protection premium would also cost its payer $20bn a year if applied to the entire balance. Protection premiums paid through the structure belong to the actual risk bearers and are not Rhino fee revenue. Borrower economics must remain viable after those premiums and all funding costs.

**4. Operating model and cost base**

Rhino's business would be a global financial institution supported by software, with substantial expenditure on people, local operations, custody arrangements, valuation, cybersecurity, legal administration and recovery management.

|---|---|---:|

| Fixed cash operating expenses | Core staff, technology, legal, compliance, audit and infrastructure | $30bn |
| Depreciation | Physical and technology infrastructure | $4bn |
| Risk planning charge | Illustrative retained-credit, counterparty, valuation and operational-loss allowance | $20bn |

The $20bn risk charge is a scenario expense, not an accounting determination or an actuarial estimate. The forecast remains provisional until loan-level exposures and operational risks support a defensible provision and valuation methodology. The model assumes no material net warehousing, hedge or operating funding cost beyond the expenses and net revenue shown. Any such additional cost reduces the forecast dollar for dollar.

**5. EBITA definition and 2035 bridge**

For this internal model, EBITA means earnings before corporate financing interest, tax and amortisation, after depreciation, operating expenses and the stated risk planning charge. It is a pro forma management measure, not a statutory bank-reporting metric. Business funding and protection costs must be reflected before evaluating the economics; excluding corporate financing interest does not make bank funding free.

Amortisation, corporate financing interest and tax would then reduce earnings further. No net-profit, dividend or equity valuation forecast is made. Required capital and the cost of retaining it remain separate investment decisions.

**7. Sensitivity and downside**

At the full $200tn average reference balance, fee compression and risk costs have very large effects:

| 2035 scenario | Downside | Reference case | Higher-fee case |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Administration fee | 6bp | 10bp | 12bp |
| Arrangement fee on $40tn | 10bp | 20bp | 30bp |
| Net fee revenue | $160bn | $280bn | $360bn |
| Variable operating expenses | $56bn (35%) | $84bn (30%) | $108bn (30%) |
| Fixed expenses including depreciation | $34bn | $34bn | $34bn |
| Risk planning charge | $40bn | $20bn | $20bn |
| **Modelled EBITA** | **$30bn** | **$142bn** | **$198bn** |

These cases hold scale constant. If average principal reaches only $100tn, with reference-case fees, 20% annual arrangements, variable expenses of 30%, $34bn of fixed expenses including depreciation, and a 1bp risk charge, EBITA falls to **$54bn**. A universal platform could also face stronger procurement and political pressure to lower fees; market share alone does not establish pricing power.

**8. Risk retention can overwhelm the franchise**

The reference case assumes investors and originators bear almost all underlying portfolio credit risk. For illustration, if Rhino retains a uniformly distributed pro rata exposure equal to 0.1% of the reference portfolio, its gross retained principal is $200bn. A 10% net credit loss across that exposure costs $20bn. At a 1% pro rata retention, the same stress costs $200bn; at 5%, it costs $1tn. These are alternative total stress losses, not incremental charges to add blindly to the existing planning allowance.

First-loss retention is materially more dangerous than those pro rata examples. A retained 0–5% first-loss band on the full reference portfolio would expose $10tn of principal; a 1% net loss on the whole pool could consume $2tn of that band, before other offsets. The fee model cannot support uncapped guarantees of global credit performance.

Actual retention obligations depend on the originator, jurisdiction and transaction. An independent arranger is not automatically required to retain a fixed percentage of every reference loan, but neither can Rhino assume every economic interest can be sold. Capital, collateral, margin liquidity, warehousing and resolution capacity must be modelled separately. This memorandum therefore gives no unsupported capital-adequacy or return-on-equity figure.

**9. Execution and governance**

The initial business should establish standardised data, enforceable contracts, transparent loss settlement and a defensible net fee on identifiable portfolios. Expansion requires evidence that each new jurisdiction and asset class adds genuine funding access or diversification rather than duplicate exposure.

By 2030, the target operating system would support multi-currency issuance, independent trustees, partner servicing and a register of ultimate risk holders. By 2032, country-level resolution plans and portable servicing would need to make the network resilient to a Rhino outage or failure. The 2035 universal-franchise scenario requires investors able to finance $40tn of annual reference arrangements and sustain the associated protection obligations; reference principal is not itself a measure of available investor cash.

The platform's durability would rest on transparent pricing, service continuity and credible loss allocation. Circular financing of tranche buyers, hidden guarantees and automatic assumptions of official rescue would undermine the business model.

**Board conclusion**

At a $200tn average reference portfolio, Rhino could support **$280bn of annual net fee revenue and $142bn of modelled EBITA** under the stated assumptions. The franchise would earn an economically small fee on a vast volume of financing, with operating scale generating the margin.

The essential discipline is to own the infrastructure, charge for measurable services, distribute risks to identified institutions and fund any retained obligations explicitly. Rhino would become the synthetic financier of the system by organising capital and risk at global scale; it would not acquire the world's output merely by issuing claims against it.
